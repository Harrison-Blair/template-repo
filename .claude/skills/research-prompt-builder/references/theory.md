# Theory / General Research Template

Use for conceptual, historical, social, cultural, scientific-consensus, or biographical research. This is the fallback when the topic doesn't fit Technical, Data-Driven, or Comparison.

Fill placeholders in `[BRACKETS]`. Mark unconfirmed defaults with `[DEFAULT — confirm]`. Leave anything the user definitely cares about but hasn't specified as `[FILL IN]`.

---

## Template (this is the prompt the user pastes into deep research)

```markdown
I want a deep research report on **[TOPIC]**.

**Audience**: [AUDIENCE — e.g., an educated generalist; or "a graduate student in this field"]
**Depth**: [DEPTH — e.g., thorough overview; or "exhaustive treatment with primary-source quotations"]
**Time scope**: [TIME — e.g., full historical context; or "focus on developments since [YEAR]"]
**Geographic / cultural scope**: [SCOPE — e.g., Western philosophical tradition; or "global, with attention to non-Anglophone scholarship"]
**Exclusions**: [EXCLUSIONS — e.g., skip popular-press summaries, avoid sources without clear provenance]

### What I want to come away understanding

[USER'S CORE QUESTION OR LEARNING GOAL — restated in one or two sentences. If the user gave you a topic without a learning goal, draft one and mark it for confirmation.]

### Structure

1. **Core concept and definition.** What is [TOPIC]? Distinguish it from neighboring concepts that are easy to confuse with it.
2. **Origins and intellectual lineage.** Where did this come from? Who developed it? What problem or prior position was it responding to?
3. **Major positions and debates.** The main schools of thought, central disagreements, points of consensus. Be specific about who holds which view.
4. **Current state.** Where does [TOPIC] stand now in [academic philosophy / historiography / public discourse / professional practice — pick the relevant arena]?
5. **Critiques and limitations.** The strongest arguments against, what [TOPIC] does not explain, where it has been superseded or where it is contested.
6. **Why it matters.** What turns on [TOPIC] being true / popular / dominant? What practical or theoretical stakes does it carry?

### Output format

Markdown document with the six items above as `##` section headings. Use `###` subheadings for sub-arguments, blockquotes for cited passages from primary sources.

Cite specific claims and quotations inline.

End with `## References`. Real, verifiable links only, with a one-sentence note for each. Prefer **primary sources** (the original work being discussed, peer-reviewed papers, established encyclopedias such as the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy or IEP for philosophical topics, major review articles, archival documents). Omit any source you are uncertain about — **never fabricate** titles, authors, dates, or URLs. If a claim cannot be confidently sourced, soften the claim rather than invent a citation.
```
